FIVE CONNECTED PRACTICES

Use these five boundaries and this order:

1. Financial Management & FP&A

Challenge:
When budgets lag behind the business, cash needs are unclear or growth puts margins under pressure, decisions need more than a spreadsheet update.

Support:
Connect financial planning and analysis with revenue, cost and resource assumptions. Cover budgeting, rolling forecasts, financial KPIs, scenario modelling, budget-versus-actual analysis, profitability and management reporting.

Typical deliverables:
A budget and rolling forecast, documented scenarios, financial KPIs, variance commentary and profitability analysis. Where appropriate, include a 13-week cash outlook.

Decision benefit:
Understand the financial implications of spending, pricing, capacity and growth before committing resources.

Do not imply regulated audit, tax or investment advice, or unverified outsourced-CFO experience.

2. Business Intelligence & Data Analytics

Challenge:
Teams report different figures, rebuild spreadsheets every month or lack confidence in their dashboards.

Support:
Map data sources, assess data quality, agree KPI definitions and investigate financial, customer, commercial and operational performance.

Typical deliverables:
A KPI dictionary, source map, data-quality findings, agreed data transformations, diagnostic analysis and a dashboard or management reporting pack with an update process.

Decision benefit:
Use consistent information to understand what changed, investigate the drivers and decide where to focus.

Clearly distinguish analytics from business analysis:
- Analytics investigates numbers, performance and drivers.
- Business analysis investigates workflows, requirements and process/system changes.

3. Business Management & Operations

Challenge:
Unclear ownership, stretched capacity and repeated handoffs make growth expensive and difficult to manage.

Support:
Review operating models, business requirements, resources, capacity, workflows and management routines.

Keep Revenue Operations and GTM processes prominent here. Explain how marketing, sales, delivery and finance need shared definitions and reliable handoffs.

Typical deliverables:
Process and ownership maps, business requirements, capacity analysis, a practical management cadence and prioritised workflow improvements.

Decision benefit:
Give teams clearer responsibilities and changes they can use and maintain.

Include a visible link to the cross-practice Revenue-to-Finance Alignment feature.

4. CRM & Commercial Systems

Challenge:
Incomplete CRM records, inconsistent pipeline stages and missed follow-ups weaken sales execution and reporting.

Support:
Provide a clear entry point for independent CRM/HubSpot support: lifecycle design, pipeline hygiene, administration, data validation, lead routing, workflows, reporting and integration assessment.

Typical deliverables:
A CRM review and improvement backlog, lifecycle and pipeline definitions, data-validation rules, scoped configuration or automation, reporting and administration documentation.

Decision benefit:
Make commercial information easier to trust and maintain, with clearer handoffs between marketing, sales, delivery and finance.

Do not claim HubSpot accreditation or partner status. Do not advertise Acumatica implementation capability without confirmed qualified capacity.

5. AI Enablement & Automation

Challenge:
Repetitive tasks and pressure to adopt AI do not establish whether an automation will be useful.

Support:
Assess workflow readiness, data, exceptions, expected costs and human-review requirements before selecting a use case.

Typical deliverables:
Prioritised use cases, a workflow/data-readiness assessment, one scoped pilot, quality checks and a measurement plan covering time, cost and adoption. Include agreed training and handover.

Decision benefit:
Help the business decide whether an improvement is useful and worth maintaining.

Do not promise guaranteed savings or autonomous high-stakes financial decisions.

METRIC EXPLORER

Add or retain editable examples with formulas, calculated arithmetic, clear definitions and lightweight proportional bars.

1. Gross margin:
(revenue − direct delivery costs) / revenue × 100.
Default: (120,000 − 48,000) / 120,000 = 60.0%.
Allow negative margins. Zero revenue is undefined.

2. Net revenue retention:
(opening cohort MRR + expansion − contraction − churn) / opening MRR × 100.
Default: (50,000 + 8,000 − 2,000 − 4,000) / 50,000 = 104.0%.
Exclude new customers.
Explain zero opening MRR and reject inconsistent cohort losses.

3. Cash runway:
available cash / (monthly payments − monthly receipts).
Default: 150,000 / (75,000 − 50,000) = 6.0 months.
Use only positive, reasonably stable net burn.
For zero or negative burn, show an explanation rather than infinite or negative runway.
