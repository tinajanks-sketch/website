REVENUE-TO-FINANCE ALIGNMENT

Connect revenue expectations with financial decisions.

A sale affects more than the pipeline. Delivery, invoicing, costs and payment timing all influence the financial picture. Explore the connections that need clear definitions and ownership.

Agree what a commercial opportunity means.

Stage definitions, expected close dates, probabilities and contract terms influence the revenue assumptions used in financial planning. Pipeline value, contracted work and recognised revenue need distinct definitions.

Connect the sale with the work needed to deliver it.

Scope, delivery milestones, staffing and external costs affect timing and profitability. Clarify which information moves from commercial teams to the people responsible for delivery.

Separate invoicing from cash received.

Billing schedules, payment terms and outstanding receivables influence cash availability. A profitable sale can still create a cash gap when payments arrive after the related costs.

Use the connected information to review the outlook.

How this example is calculated

Forecast revenue starts from the latest actual month and compounds at the selected monthly growth rate. Variable costs are the selected share of forecast revenue.

Forecast fixed overheads carry forward the latest actual month, adjusted by the selected percentage. Operating result equals revenue minus variable costs and fixed overheads.

The original budget assumes monthly revenue growth of 2%, variable costs of 40% and fixed overheads of EUR 42,000. Actual figures are a separate, fixed sample dataset. All calculations round to whole euros each month. Fixed overheads are held constant after adjustment; seasonality, hiring and capacity constraints are not modelled. This simplified model excludes taxes, financing and the timing of cash collections or payments; operating result is not cash flow.

All figures are hypothetical and illustrate the method. They are not client results or a forecast for your business.
