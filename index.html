STRATEGIC FINANCE · REVENUE-TO-FINANCE

A clearer connection between growth and financial performance.

A sales opportunity, signed booking, recognised revenue, issued invoice and received payment are different measures. Explore how information moves between commercial and finance teams, and why timing, margins and ownership matter when planning ahead.

An opportunity is not yet an order.

Pipeline records potential business. Bookings record committed contracts under an agreed definition; they do not automatically represent recognised revenue or cash. Stage quality, expected close dates, scope and payment terms shape the assumptions handed to operations and finance.

Delivery and revenue recognition need a defined basis.

Service periods, milestones, capacity and delivery costs influence performance and margin. Revenue recognition depends on the contract and the applicable accounting policy; neither an order nor an invoice alone establishes when revenue is earned. Agree the information and ownership needed for that assessment.

An invoice creates a collection task, not a bank receipt.

Invoicing schedules, credit notes and outstanding receivables affect what is due and when. Track invoice dates, due dates and overdue balances separately from recognised revenue. A deposit can arrive before delivery; a customer payment can arrive well after revenue is recognised.

Cash timing completes the planning picture.

A customer payment increases available bank cash when it is received. Combine collection assumptions with payroll, suppliers and other payments in a cash outlook, while keeping the P&L view separate. Revenue operations, analytics and clear system handoffs help teams reconcile these views and understand gaps.

Explanatory flow, not a universal accounting sequence. Contract terms, business models and accounting policies differ. The P&L forecast below does not calculate cash balances.

How this example is calculated

Forecast revenue starts from the latest actual month and compounds at the selected monthly growth rate. Variable costs are the selected share of forecast revenue.

Forecast fixed overheads carry forward the latest actual month, adjusted by the selected percentage. Operating result equals revenue minus variable costs and fixed overheads.

The original budget assumes monthly revenue growth of 2%, variable costs of 40% and fixed overheads of EUR 42,000. Actual figures are a separate, fixed sample dataset. All calculations round to whole euros each month. Fixed overheads are held constant after adjustment; seasonality, hiring and capacity constraints are not modelled. This simplified model excludes taxes, financing and the timing of cash collections or payments; operating result is not cash flow.

All figures are hypothetical and illustrate the method. They are not client results or a forecast for your business.
